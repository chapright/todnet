## Critique — FoxyVPN web interface (`foxy-windows/web`)

Assessed: `web/index.html`, `web/css/*`, `web/js/**`, the 33 committed screenshots in `docs/`, and the live interface. ### Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The material and the voice are authored; the composition is not. Ember earns its name: warm graphite surfaces, one orange that behaves as emitted light, a connected orb that is a lit core rather than a lamp, and a density (13–14 px text, 32–44 px controls, 13 px rows) that reads as a Windows instrument instead of a marketing page. The state vocabulary — idle / busy / ok / error carried consistently into the title bar, the chip, the orb and the live region — is the kind of thing a neighbouring VPN client would not have. The Persian build is the real differentiator: it is mirrored by construction (logical properties), it uses a bundled Persian face with Latin technical terms left alone, it sorts countries by their Persian names, and it isolates Latin values inside RTL sentences. That is not a translation layer.

What is category-interchangeable is the skeleton: rail, a central connect orb, a right-hand stack of Exit IP / Session / Allowance cards, a Recommended row at the top of the location list. NordVPN, Proton and half of the category use this plan. Ember makes it better looking, not different. The two places where the "generic AI dark dashboard" reading becomes possible are exactly the two places the material thins: the ambient orange glow behind the stage (whose clipped edge is visible at the bottom of the tile) and the light-theme orb, which is a flat pale-peach disc — the one thing DESIGN.md explicitly forbids.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect web` returns 14 findings in four clusters. `design-system-color` ×4 is the only cluster that is a genuine defect: four hard-coded colours (`components.css:310`, `views.css:489/491`) break the project's own Closed Palette Rule, and three of the four sit in the light-theme orb — the same element that breaks the Lit-Not-Painted Rule. `design-system-radius` ×2 (1 px on the signal bars, 2 px on the location selection bar) and `bounce-easing` ×4 (`--ease-spring`, which DESIGN.md authorises for the nav pill, switch, segmented thumb, toast and modal) are false positives by the project's own written system. `cramped-padding` on `#titlebar`, `marquee` on the skeleton shimmer, `dark-glow` on `.orb-core` and `radial-halo` on the stage are intentional-world or wrong-shape: the shimmer is a loading placeholder, and the glow is the product's committed light source, suppressed entirely when connected.

**Live overlay.** In the harness tab the detector ran in the page and reported 3 findings: `tiny-text` on `#titlebarStateText` (11 px) and on `#totalVal.metric-total` (11 px), plus `dark-glow` on `.orb-core`. The title-bar line is the sanctioned micro tier; the session totals are not — they are measurements set below the readout tier, which the detector is right to flag. The overlay is visible in the tab that is still open; the live server was stopped afterwards (`live-server stop --keep-inject`).

### Overall Impression

This is a disciplined, unusually literate piece of interface work with a real material identity, and it is let down by the three moments that matter most in a VPN client: the first five seconds (nothing explains what this is or that it is unofficial), the smallest supported window (the button you came for is off-screen), and the failure that cannot be retried (the interface still offers Retry). The single biggest opportunity: make the failure and first-run surfaces as honest as the connected one already is.

### What's Working

1. **State that never overclaims.** The title bar, chip, orb and live region say the same four words, the exit IP is repeated on the stage as a pill, and "Verified in 0.4 s" is real evidence rather than reassurance. The verifying state even shows a skeleton where the address will land.
2. **Measured, not asserted, contrast.** Live measurement of the rendered views found a lowest text ratio of 4.85:1 on Locations (`100 ms` at 11 px) and 5.53:1 for the smallest captions everywhere else; the primary button is 7.38:1. The "measured contrast" claim survives contact with the running app.
3. **Accessibility that is structural.** Every one of the 34 interactive elements on Settings has an accessible name; switches are `role="switch"` with `aria-checked`; segmented controls carry `aria-pressed`; the rail carries `aria-current`; modals have `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, a focus trap, Escape and focus return; the popover supports arrow keys. This is not a bolt-on.

### Priority Issues

**[P1] At the minimum supported window, the primary action is off-screen.**
- **What:** At 760×560 (the documented minimum) the orb, headline, subline and exit-IP pill consume the stage; Disconnect/Cancel/Retry and the location picker sit below the fold, and the exit-IP pill breaks onto two lines ("185.152.65.42 ·" / "Netherlands"). In Persian the subline wraps to three lines and strands the duration. The account card collapses to an unlabelled avatar.
- **Why it matters:** Home is the entire product; at a size Windows users reach by snapping, the one control they need is not visible and the views have no visible scrollbar.
- **Fix:** Give the stage a bounded vertical rhythm (orb `clamp()`, headline/subline fixed lines) and pin the action group to the bottom of the tile at short heights; keep the pill on one line by shrinking the country name before the address; give the account card a tooltip when compact.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Failure surfaces do not adapt to the cause.**
- **What:** With the allowance exhausted, the banner correctly says "Monthly allowance used up", but the stage still says "Could not connect" and offers "Retry" plus a location picker — neither can work. On a generic failure the same operation appears twice with two names: "Try again" in the banner and "Retry" on the stage, while the stage carries no cause of its own.
- **Why it matters:** This is the moment trust is won or lost; an affordance that cannot succeed teaches the user the app is guessing.
- **Fix:** Add an `exhausted` state to `describeState`/`updateAction` (primary "Accounts", no location chip, headline "Allowance used up"), and on generic failure let the banner own the action while the stage states the cause and keeps only Details.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Persian breaks in two places that English does not.**
- **What:** On Logs at 1000 px the toolbar overflows: "دنبال کردن" drops to a second row and "پاک کردن" loses its button entirely, rendering as loose text over the log panel. On Locations the search field renders no magnifier at all (the span sits at x 733 inside the field, visible in LTR, absent in RTL), while the Logs field shows one.
- **Why it matters:** Persian is declared first-class; these are a core view and a primary control, not an edge case.
- **Fix:** Let the logs toolbar wrap as groups (or collapse the two icon actions into a menu) with `min-inline-size: 0` on each group; give the location search the same direction treatment as the logs field and verify the icon paints in RTL.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] The first run never says what the app is, and the "unofficial" disclaimer is absent from the interface.**
- **What:** The signed-out screen offers "Sign in with Firefox" and "Create an account" with a fox mark and no sentence explaining that FoxyVPN is independent, unofficial, or that it uses the free 50 GB included with a Firefox account. No welcome surface exists any more; no About or disclaimer string exists anywhere in `i18n.js` (the disclaimer lives only in the README).
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md commits to "never imply affiliation with, endorsement by, or support from Mozilla" and to keeping the disclaimer; the interface currently does the opposite by silence, and the user must trust a stranger's binary with their Firefox password.
- **Fix:** A compact first-run block above the two buttons: what FoxyVPN is, the free 50 GB via your own Firefox account, "unofficial, not affiliated with Mozilla", and a link to the README/disclaimer. Keep it reopenable from About in Settings.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

**[P2] Wide windows lose the composition.**
- **What:** At 1600×900 the session card stretches to ~360 px and leaves most of it empty beneath a 160 px sparkline; the Disconnect button drifts ~150 px below the stage text; the ambient glow's clipped edge shows as a band across the bottom of the tile in the idle and connecting states.
- **Why it matters:** A maximised desktop app is a normal state, not an edge case, and the void makes the instrument look unfinished at the size most people use.
- **Fix:** Cap the stack's row growth (`align-content: start`, `max-height` on the session card), anchor the stage's action group to the bottom of its own tile rather than the grid, and fade the ambient with a mask so its box never shows.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user).** Ctrl+1…4 and Ctrl+F work but appear nowhere — no tooltip, no menu, no hint. There is no accelerator for the only action that matters (connect/disconnect), so a keyboard user must tab through the rail to reach it. Ctrl+F on Home silently navigates to Locations, which reads as a bug, not a shortcut.

**Jordan (first-timer).** The first screen names an action ("Sign in with Firefox") before naming the product's value; nothing says "free", "50 GB" or "not made by Mozilla". The connected screen then shows "System proxy", `h2` and "TUN" with no explanation, and there is no help or About anywhere. Jordan has to guess at step one and cannot look anything up at step two.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent).** Mostly well served — focus trap, Escape, names, live regions, visible rings. Two gaps: at 760×560 the primary action is below the fold while focus can still move to it, so keyboard users lose the focus rectangle off-screen; and the account button's accessible name starts with the avatar's letter ("a alex@example.com 30.8 GB left").

**Reza (Persian user on a restricted network).** The RTL build is genuinely mirrored and the Persian copy reads naturally, but the Logs toolbar is visibly broken, the Locations search has lost its icon, and the location list shows 1 170 ms edges beside 80 ms ones with the same three-bar vocabulary — no hint that a high-latency edge is still usable, or that it came from Mozilla's own list.

### Minor Observations

- **Light theme orb contradicts the system.** In light mode the connected core is a flat pale-peach disc with a thin rim, which is exactly the "solid orange disc reads as an alarm" outcome DESIGN.md forbids. The three undocumented literals the detector found live in that element; rebuild it from tones plus a rim instead of paint.
- **Session totals are 11 px** (`#totalVal`), below the readout tier for data that changes every second. The detector agrees.
- **Hidden tiles** are reversible only through the same small chevron that hid them, and hiding removes the Exit IP card — the proof DESIGN.md says must stay visible.
- **No skeleton in the sparkline area** while verifying; the card is a large empty block next to a card that does show a skeleton.
- **The transport is `h2` on Home and `HTTP/2` in Settings** — machine token where a label belongs.
- **"Recommended — Fastest available server, chosen automatically"** is contradicted by the list beneath it (Hamburg 80 ms, Recommended 189 ms). PRODUCT.md already flags this as open; rename it to describe the anycast edge.
- **`docs/*.png` are stale** relative to the code (the error stage's subline, the low-quota account value). They are the design's evidence base, so regenerate them when the next fix lands.
- Small: 1 px radii on the 3 px signal bars and 2 px on the selection bar are outside the documented radius scale but visually correct; the `flag-chip` light shadow also uses raw literals.
- The log's Clear action has no visible confirmation of its own beyond the toast; the empty state for a filtered log existed but could not be verified here.

### Questions to Consider

- If the first run had to sell this in one sentence, what would it be — and why isn't that sentence on the screen?
- Should the failure stage ever show a primary action, or is the banner the only honest place for one?
- What would the minimum window look like if the orb were allowed to shrink instead of the button being allowed to leave?
- Is the light theme allowed to be a different room, or must it be the same room with the lights on? The orb currently answers "neither".
- If "Recommended" cannot be measured, is it a location at all?
